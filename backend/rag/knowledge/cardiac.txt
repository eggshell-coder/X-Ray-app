[Cardiac Pathology]

Key sign:
- Cardiomegaly (enlarged cardiac silhouette, cardiothoracic ratio > 0.5)
- Pulmonary vascular congestion
- Pleural effusion (blunted costophrenic angle)
- Pulmonary edema pattern (bat-wing appearance in advanced cases)

Region to inspect:
- Cardiac silhouette (center of chest)
- Bilateral lung bases (for fluid/congestion signs)

Why this region:
An enlarged heart pushes the cardiothoracic ratio beyond the normal threshold.
Fluid backing up from reduced cardiac function accumulates first at the
gravity-dependent lower lung fields, producing the congestion pattern seen
at the bases.

Model reliability note:
Cardiac Pathology is the class most frequently confused with Normal in this
model (3 of 14 test cases misclassified as Normal). This reflects genuine
anatomical ambiguity — borderline cardiomegaly sits on a continuum with
normal heart size, making this the hardest boundary for the model to draw
cleanly. Error pattern is symmetric (FNR = FDR = 0.314), indicating weak
separation rather than a directional bias — the model misses Cardiac cases
and raises false Cardiac alarms at equal rates. Model predictions for this
class, especially near the confidence threshold, warrant closer radiologist
correlation than other classes.
