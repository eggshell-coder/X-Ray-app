[Pleural Pathology]

Key sign:
- Homogeneous opacification
- Blunted costophrenic angle
- Fluid meniscus sign at lung base

Region to inspect:
- Lower lateral lung field
- Pleural space, costophrenic angle

Why this region:
Pleural fluid accumulates at the gravity-dependent lung base first, producing
a characteristic homogeneous opacity with a curved upper border (meniscus)
at the lateral costophrenic angle.

Model reliability note:
Achieved perfect recall (5/5) on the held-out test set, reflecting the
high-contrast, spatially localized nature of this pattern. Precision is lower
(0.71) because some Chronic Lung Disease cases are misclassified as Pleural,
as both conditions can produce overlapping opacification patterns in the
lower lung zones — a genuinely difficult discrimination even for experienced
radiologists. False positives from this class are clinically low-risk, since
they typically prompt confirmatory imaging rather than a missed finding.

Metrics (held-out test set, n=57, support=5):
Precision 0.71 | Recall 1.00 | F1 0.83 | Specificity 0.972 | AUC 0.996

Metrics (pooled cross-validation, support=44):
Sensitivity 0.909 (95% CI 0.788-0.964) | Specificity 0.972 (0.953-0.984)
PPV 0.755 (0.624-0.851) | NPV 0.991 (0.978-0.997)
FDR 0.258 — confirms an over-calling pattern (F2=0.878 vs F0.5=0.772):
the model favors catching true Pleural cases at the cost of some false
alarms, consistent with the precision/recall trade-off above.
